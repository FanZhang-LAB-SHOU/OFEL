Runze Wang is a professional master’s student in Fishery Development at Shanghai Ocean University, advised by Professor Fan Zhang, with graduation expected in June 2027. His research examines uncertainty in fisheries stock assessment and management, including ensemble modelling, changing recruitment dynamics and management strategy evaluation.

His work combines simulation–estimation experiments in R and Stock Synthesis 3 with predictive cross-validation and time-varying stock–recruitment modelling. As a co-first author of a study published in Fish and Fisheries, he examined uncertainty specifications in 133 assessments covering seven tuna species across five regional fisheries management organisations.

Research experience

Nov 2025–Present

Ensemble modelling and model weighting

Designed and implemented an operating-model and estimation-model simulation framework in R and Stock Synthesis 3 to compare ensemble weighting methods under natural-mortality misspecification.

Implemented predictive weighting based on cross-validation and evaluated estimation bias and uncertainty calibration across scenarios.

Sep 2024–Oct 2025

Global tuna assessment uncertainty review

Examined uncertainty specifications in 133 assessments covering seven tuna species across five tuna Regional Fisheries Management Organizations, with André E. Punt and other coauthors. Contributed to study design, data compilation, comparative analysis, interpretation, and manuscript preparation.

Found greater similarity in uncertainty specifications within management organizations than within species.

Oct 2023–Present

Time-varying stock–recruitment modelling and environmental effects

Developed likelihood-based time-varying stock–recruitment models for Atlantic tropical tunas to examine changes in productivity.

Developing a study of whether environmental information improves recruitment forecasts beyond temporal benchmarks, accounting for assessment uncertainty and information available at the time of prediction.
