Runze Wang reveals how differently tuna assessments around the world handle model uncertainty

A review of 133 stock assessments from five tuna management organisations shows that choices about model uncertainty follow the organisation more than the species, and proposes best practices to make them more consistent.

Research updates

Lab members: Runze WangSisong DongFan Zhang

OFEL master's student Runze Wang has produced the first systematic analysis of how model uncertainty is specified in tuna stock assessments worldwide. The study shows that these choices vary widely, that they depend more on the management organisation than on the biology of the species, and that subjective judgement plays a substantial role. It is published in Fish and Fisheries.

Why uncertainty specification matters

Tuna are among the world's most important marine fishery resources, and their sustainable use depends on sound stock assessments. Model uncertainty is a major source of scientific uncertainty in those assessments. It is usually explored through sensitivity analyses and model ensembles, but which factors are included, and at what levels, differs both within and among the tuna Regional Fisheries Management Organisations (tRFMOs).

133 assessments, 20 years, five organisations

Runze and colleagues reviewed the uncertainty specifications in 133 stock assessments carried out over the past 20 years by five tRFMOs, covering seven major commercial tuna species. They compared which uncertainty factors each assessment included, how similar assessments were to one another, and what explained changes between successive assessments of the same stock.

What they found

Although a wide range of uncertainty specifications has been used, only a few factors are considered in most assessments. Patterns line up more closely with the management organisation than with the species: assessments within one organisation tend to look alike even for different tunas, suggesting that institutional practice shapes these choices more than biology does.

The emphasis has also shifted over time. Uncertainty related to data, such as data weighting, has received less attention, while biological processes such as growth have received more. Between successive assessments of the same stock, changes in modelling approach, along with changes in fleet structure and stock definitions, were the main reasons the uncertainty specification changed.

Towards more consistent assessments

The authors recommend cross-organisation coordination and expert review to make assessment methods more consistent and transparent, together with a standardised checklist of uncertainties so that every potential source is considered and individual analysts' preferences carry less weight. They also set out best-practice guidance that walks assessment teams step by step through selecting and specifying uncertainty.

Runze Wang and postdoctoral researcher Sisong Dong are co-first authors, and Professor Fan Zhang is the corresponding author. Co-authors include André E. Punt of the University of Washington and Jiangfeng Zhu of Shanghai Ocean University.
